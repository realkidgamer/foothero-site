His Heart Stopped On Live Television. Ten Months Later, He Scored.

FootHero Stories · Christian Eriksen · 6 min read

On a June evening in Copenhagen, Christian Eriksen collapsed in the middle of a European Championship match and his heart stopped beating. What happened next — on the pitch, and in the months that followed — is one of the most remarkable survival stories the sport has ever produced.

It happened during Denmark's opening match of Euro 2020 against Finland at Parken Stadium, just before halftime. Eriksen went to receive a throw-in, then collapsed face-first onto the grass without contact from anyone. Within seconds, Denmark's captain Simon Kjær was at his side, recognizing what was happening, checking his airway, and turning him into the recovery position while medical staff sprinted onto the field.

Eriksen had suffered a cardiac arrest. Team doctors performed CPR and used a defibrillator on the pitch, in full view of the stadium and a live television audience across the continent. Kjær, meanwhile, organized his teammates into a human wall to shield Eriksen from the cameras and to comfort his wife, Sabrina, who had run down from the stands. It remains one of the most widely praised acts of sportsmanship in the tournament's history.

He was resuscitated on the field and rushed to a Copenhagen hospital, where doctors confirmed he was stable. With the players' agreement, the match was completed later that same evening; Denmark lost, but the result mattered to almost no one watching.

Eriksen was alive, and conscious, and asking about his teammates before he'd even left the stadium.

Doctors fitted him with an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator, a small device that monitors heart rhythm and can deliver a shock if it detects another arrest. It likely saved his career from ending there — but not immediately. Italian football's medical regulations at the time did not permit players with an ICD to compete, and his club, Inter Milan, terminated his contract by mutual consent that December so he could continue playing elsewhere.

Eriksen signed with Brentford in the Premier League in early 2022, where the device carried no such restriction. He returned to competitive football that February, wearing the number 10, in a home match against Newcastle United — scoring a goal in the game and helping the club to a win in only his first appearance back. By the end of the year he had signed for Manchester United and traveled to Qatar with Denmark for the World Cup, playing at the tournament he'd nearly never seen again.

There is no explaining away what happened to Christian Eriksen that night in Copenhagen — only the fact that he survived it, and chose to come back anyway. Few players have ever had more reason to walk away from the game. He didn't.
