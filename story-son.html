17 Years Without A Trophy. Their Captain Finally Ended It.

FootHero Stories · Son Heung-min · 6 min read

Tottenham Hotspur had not won a major trophy since 2008. For seventeen years, that drought became one of the running jokes of English football — a club with money, history, and star players that somehow never won anything. Son Heung-min, the longest-serving player in the squad, had lived through nearly all of it. In Bilbao in May 2025, he finally got to stop waiting.

Son signed for Tottenham from Bayer Leverkusen in 2015, then a club-record transfer fee, arriving as a promising but unproven winger from the Bundesliga. He grew into one of the most reliable forwards in the Premier League, sharing the Golden Boot in 2021–22 with Mohamed Salah and forming a devastating partnership with Harry Kane. He was named club captain. What he hadn't done, in nearly a decade at the club, was lift a trophy with it.

That wasn't for lack of trying. Tottenham reached a Champions League final in 2019 and lost to Liverpool. They reached a League Cup final in 2021 and lost to Manchester City. Every near miss added another layer to the joke — a punchline about a club that always seemed to find a new way to fall just short. Fans had stopped expecting it to end.

The 2024–25 season looked, for most of its length, like more of the same, only worse. Injuries tore through the squad. The team's Premier League form collapsed to the point they finished in the bottom half of the table, one of the worst domestic campaigns in the club's recent history. Manager Ange Postecoglou's methods and Son's captaincy were both openly questioned by supporters and pundits. Almost the only thing keeping the season alive was a Europa League run nobody had predicted would matter this much — built, in part, on Postecoglou's own half-joking promise that he always delivers a trophy in his second season at a club.

It ended with him lifting a trophy anyway. Tottenham reached the Europa League final and faced Manchester United in Bilbao — an all-English final between two clubs having such miserable league seasons that the match was, only half-jokingly, billed as the one trophy either of them could still win that year. Brennan Johnson scored the only goal of the game in the first half, and Tottenham spent the rest of the night defending a single-goal lead against a United side that kept pushing for an equalizer.

It ended with him lifting a trophy anyway.

They held on. Deep into stoppage time, with United still probing for a way back in, Micky van de Ven made a goal-line clearance that preserved the lead in the tensest moment of the match. Moments later, the referee blew the final whistle, and Tottenham Hotspur were champions of Europe for the first time since the 1984 UEFA Cup — and winners of a major trophy for the first time since 2008.

The win also secured Tottenham's return to the Champions League, a competition their league form alone would never have qualified them for that season. It was a strange, lopsided year — a club that struggled domestically all the way to a European trophy — and Son, who had absorbed criticism all season as captain, was the one holding the cup up at the final whistle.

The story didn't stay tidy for long. Postecoglou was dismissed just weeks later, despite delivering the trophy he'd promised, after a Premier League season the club decided it couldn't build on. But nothing that came after changed what happened on the pitch in Bilbao: after seventeen years of near misses and jokes about the club's luck, the captain who had waited longest finally got to lift something.
