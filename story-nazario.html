His Knee Exploded On Live Television. Two Years Later, He Won The World Cup.

FootHero Stories · Ronaldo Nazário · 6 min read

In April 2000, Ronaldo Nazário's right knee gave out completely in the opening minutes of a match in Rome, his kneecap tendon tearing apart in front of a stadium that fell silent. It was the second time in a year his knee had failed him. Doctors, teammates, and Ronaldo himself wondered if he'd ever play again. Two years later, he scored both goals in a World Cup final.

By the time he was 21, Ronaldo was already being called the best player in the world. He'd won FIFA's World Player of the Year award twice, led Barcelona to a Cup Winners' Cup, and moved to Inter Milan for a world record fee. His combination of speed, balance, and finishing had no real precedent — commentators struggled to compare him to anyone who'd played before.

Then, in November 1999, playing for Inter against Lecce, his right knee buckled under him with no contact from another player. Surgeons found a torn patellar tendon and operated. He spent months rebuilding it, working through a rehabilitation program that stretched into the following spring. Inter, and Ronaldo, treated his return as a milestone worth celebrating.

He lasted six minutes. In his comeback match, an Italian Cup final against Lazio in April 2000, the same tendon snapped again — this time completely, in front of the cameras, as he tried to control the ball near the touchline. He went down clutching his knee and had to be carried off. The injury was severe enough that some inside the game quietly wrote off the rest of his career.

He spent nearly a thousand days waiting for his knee to trust him again.

What followed was one of the longest and most uncertain rehabilitations in modern football. Ronaldo barely played across the next two seasons, in and out of an Inter side that had built its ambitions around him. There were false starts, minutes here and there, and a knee that he later admitted he no longer trusted to plant and turn the way it once had. Plenty of players never come back from one ruptured patellar tendon. He'd had two.

He kept working anyway, rebuilding the muscles around the joint almost from scratch, changing how he moved to protect it. By the time the 2002 World Cup arrived in South Korea and Japan, Ronaldo had barely a full season of football in his legs over the previous two years. Brazil's coaching staff picked him regardless, betting a World Cup campaign on a knee that had failed twice already.

He scored in the group stage. He scored in the round of 16, the quarterfinal, and the semifinal. By the final against Germany, he had matched his own history: two goals, both his, in a match Brazil won 2-0 to claim a record fifth World Cup title. He finished the tournament with eight goals, the most of any player, and won the Golden Boot to go with the trophy. Two years after being carried off a pitch in Rome, wondering if his knee would ever hold again, he had just played the tournament of his life.
