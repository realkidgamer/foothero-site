He Had Never Scored A Goal In His Life. Then He Did It For His Father.

FootHero Stories · Alisson Becker · 5 min read

Goalkeepers are not supposed to score. It is the one certainty of their job — they exist to stop the ball from crossing a line, not to send it over one themselves. Alisson Becker had spent his entire senior career proving that rule true. Then, three months after burying his father, he broke it in the most improbable way imaginable.

By the spring of 2021, Alisson had made hundreds of professional appearances for Internacional, Roma, and Liverpool without ever scoring a single goal. That wasn't a slump or bad luck — it's simply what being a goalkeeper means. A shot-stopper who finds the net in a competitive match does something that happens to almost nobody who has ever played the position, at any level, anywhere in the world.

In February 2021, Alisson's father, José Agostinho Becker, drowned in a lake near the family's home in southern Brazil. Alisson flew back immediately, missing matches to grieve with his family in the town where he'd grown up dreaming of playing professional football. He returned to Liverpool afterward carrying something heavier than the form slump the team was already fighting through that season.

Liverpool's title defense had unraveled under a wave of injuries, and by mid-May they were fighting simply to finish high enough to qualify for the following season's Champions League. Every match mattered. On the 16th of that month, they hosted West Bromwich Albion at Anfield in their final home game of the campaign, knowing a dropped point could cost them a place in Europe's top competition.

Mohamed Salah put Liverpool ahead, West Brom equalized, and the minutes kept ticking away with the game locked at 1-1. Deep into stoppage time, with a corner won and nothing left to lose, Alisson jogged the length of the pitch and joined the attack — a goalkeeper standing among strikers and centre-backs, waiting for a ball to drop from the sky.

A shot-stopper who finds the net in a competitive match does something that happens to almost nobody who has ever played the position.

The delivery swung in, and Alisson rose above a crowd of defenders who had no idea what to do with a goalkeeper standing in their box. He met it clean and powered a header into the net. Anfield, already loud, seemed to detonate. Teammates piled on top of him in disbelief. Alisson pointed to the sky, then he wept.

He said afterward that the goal was for his father — a release built from three months of grief that had nowhere else to go until a football fell out of the sky in front of him. Liverpool held on to win 2-1, kept their European hopes alive, and went on to secure Champions League qualification by the end of that season. None of it would be remembered the same way without the header that made it possible, or the story of the man who scored it.
