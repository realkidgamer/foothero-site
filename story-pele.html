He Was Too Injured To Start. He Left Sweden As The Youngest World Champion Ever.

FootHero Stories · Pelé · 6 min read

Pelé arrived at the 1958 World Cup as a seventeen-year-old nobody outside Brazil had heard of, carrying a knee injury that kept him out of the opening two matches. By the final whistle in Stockholm, he was a World Cup winner, a World Cup final scorer, and the most talked-about teenager in the history of the sport.

He grew up in Bauru, in the Brazilian state of São Paulo, in a family with little money. As a boy he shined shoes and played barefoot in the streets with a ball made of rags, the way an entire generation of Brazilian kids did. By his mid-teens he was already turning heads at Santos FC, the club that had signed him at fifteen. Brazil's national team took a chance on him for the 1958 World Cup in Sweden, even though almost nobody beyond South America knew his name.

The tournament didn't start the way anyone at Santos had hoped. A knee injury kept Pelé out of Brazil's first two group games, and there was real doubt about whether the teenager would play a meaningful role at all. He finally made his World Cup debut in the third group match, against the Soviet Union, and did enough to convince the coaching staff he belonged on the pitch for what came next.

What came next changed the way the world thought about him. In the quarterfinal against Wales, with the match still scoreless deep into the second half, Pelé scored the only goal of the game — becoming, at seventeen, the youngest goalscorer in World Cup history up to that point. In the semifinal against France, he did something no player had done on that stage before: he scored a hat-trick, leading Brazil to a 5-2 win and a place in the final.

A teenager who'd been too injured to start the tournament was now two matches from immortality.

The final was played in Stockholm against the host nation, in front of a crowd loudly behind Sweden. Brazil fell behind early, then answered it and took control. Pelé scored twice, including a goal that footage of the match still shows being replayed decades later — he took a high ball on his chest inside the box, flicked it up and over a defender in one motion, and volleyed it past the goalkeeper before it touched the ground. Brazil won 5-2, lifting the country's first World Cup title.

What happened after the final whistle became as famous as anything that happened during it. Pelé, overwhelmed, broke down in tears on the pitch and had to be comforted by his own teammates before he could collect himself enough to celebrate. He was still a teenager who, months earlier, had not even been certain of his place in the squad. He left Sweden as the youngest player to score in a World Cup final and the youngest World Cup winner in the tournament's history — a record that has never been threatened since.

It was the first of three World Cups Pelé would go on to win with Brazil, and the moment that turned a teenager from Bauru into a name the entire footballing world would know for the rest of his life.
