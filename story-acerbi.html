He Beat Cancer Twice. Then He Played a Champions League Final.

FootHero Stories · Francesco Acerbi · 6 min read

In 2013, Francesco Acerbi thought his career might be over before it really started. Twelve years later, he was walking out for his second Champions League final — a journey almost nobody could have scripted.

Acerbi had just signed with Sassuolo when a routine pre-season blood test flagged something unusual. Doctors found testicular cancer. Surgeons removed the tumor immediately, and within months he was back in training, determined to put the diagnosis behind him.

It didn't stay behind him. A follow-up test later that year came back abnormal again — the cancer had returned. This time there was no quick fix. Acerbi began an intense course of chemotherapy, reportedly still showing up to train with his teammates whenever he could manage it, even as the treatment wore him down.

What makes Acerbi's story land so hard isn't just the illness itself. The years around it were brutal in other ways too — he'd lost his father to long-term heart problems the year before, and by his own account, fell into a period of heavy drinking and depression that nearly pushed him out of football entirely.

He came through all of it. Acerbi returned for the 2014-15 season, rebuilt his career at Lazio, and eventually became a mainstay of Italy's national team, helping the Azzurri lift the European Championship in 2021. By 2022 he'd joined Inter Milan, where he became one of the club's most trusted defenders.

"Without cancer, I would have retired at 28."

In 2023, Acerbi played every minute of Inter's Champions League final defeat to Manchester City. It looked like it might be the closest he'd ever get to the top of European football. Then, in May 2025, with Inter trailing Barcelona in a chaotic Champions League semi-final and staring at elimination, the 37-year-old defender pushed forward, chipped the ball past two Barcelona defenders, and scored the equalizer that sent the match to extra time. Inter went on to win and reach a second final in three seasons.

Inter were beaten heavily in that final, but for Acerbi, simply being there again — twelve years after a diagnosis that once made retirement feel like the only realistic outcome — was its own kind of victory. He has said since that the illness didn't just interrupt his career. It gave him one.
