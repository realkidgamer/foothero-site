Two Minutes From Elimination. She Headed The U.S. Into The Semifinals.

FootHero Stories · Abby Wambach · 5 min read

With less than two minutes left in extra time, the United States women's national team was headed out of the 2011 World Cup — a player down, a goal down, and out of answers against the most feared attacker on the planet. Then Abby Wambach jumped higher than everyone else on the pitch, and turned near-elimination into one of the most replayed goals in the history of the tournament.

The quarterfinal against Brazil, played in Dresden on July 10, 2011, started well enough for the Americans. Brazilian defender Daiane turned a cross into her own net inside the first two minutes, and the U.S. carried a 1-0 lead into halftime. But Brazil — built around Marta, already a two-time FIFA World Player of the Year — kept pressing, and the game turned hard in the second half.

In the 68th minute, U.S. defender Rachel Buehler brought down Marta inside the box. The referee pointed to the spot and sent Buehler off, leaving the Americans to play the rest of the match with ten players. Goalkeeper Hope Solo saved Cristiane's resulting penalty, but the referee ordered a retake for encroachment. Marta didn't miss twice. The score was level at 1-1, and stayed that way through ninety minutes.

Extra time only made things harder for the shorthanded Americans. Two minutes in, Marta collected a cross at the back post and finished with a flicked half-volley so good it's still shown today as one of the great individual goals in Women's World Cup history. Brazil led 2-1. The U.S. had a player disadvantage, a goal to find, and less than half an hour left to find it.

Then, deep into stoppage time at the end of extra time — the 122nd minute, by the official clock — Megan Rapinoe won the ball on the left wing and drove a cross toward the back post under pressure. Wambach met it with a header that beat the goalkeeper clean. It was, at the time, the latest goal ever scored in a Women's World Cup match, and it sent the game to penalties with a scoreline that read, almost unbelievably, 2-2.

The U.S. won the shootout 5-3, with Solo saving two of Brazil's attempts to seal it. In the years since, the goal has repeatedly been voted among the greatest moments in Women's World Cup history. The Americans rode the momentum from that night all the way to the final in Frankfurt, where they eventually fell to Japan on penalties — but the header that kept the run alive, scored a player down and a minute from elimination, is still the moment people bring up first.

Wambach barely touched the ball for most of that match. She spent extra time watching her team fall further behind, a player short against one of the most gifted attackers the sport has ever produced. None of that mattered once Rapinoe's cross was in the air. She just went up and got it.
