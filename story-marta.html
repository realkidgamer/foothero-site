Born Into A Ban. Became The Record.

FootHero Stories · Marta · 6 min read

For nearly four decades, it was illegal for a woman to play football in Brazil. Marta was born seven years after that ban was lifted — and grew up to become the leading goal-scorer, man or woman, in World Cup history.

She grew up in Dois Riachos, a small, drought-prone town in the Brazilian interior, in a family that had little money and, for long stretches, no father in the house. What she lacked in resources, Marta made up for in hours spent on a dirt lot near her home, playing barefoot in pickup games against boys because there was no girls' team for her to join. She was often the only girl on the field, and just as often the best player on it — which didn't stop people from telling her she had no business being there.

That resistance had a legal history behind it. From 1941 until 1979, Brazil's government formally barred women from playing sports it deemed "incompatible with their nature," football chief among them. The ban was lifted five years before Marta was born, but the attitudes behind it didn't disappear with the law. She grew up being mocked and occasionally chased off the same fields where boys her age were being scouted by professional clubs.

She was often the only girl on the field, and just as often the best player on it.

At fourteen, she left Dois Riachos for Rio de Janeiro on the strength of a phone call and a tryout invitation from Vasco da Gama's women's team, unsure whether she'd even make the squad. She made it, and within a few years she was in a Brazil shirt. Her club career soon carried her further still — to Sweden's Umeå IK in 2004, where she won domestic titles and a UEFA Women's Cup, and later through the early years of professional women's soccer in the United States, including a long run with the Orlando Pride. Wherever she went, the goals went with her.

Between 2006 and 2010, FIFA named her the best women's player on the planet five years in a row. She won the award again in 2018, for six in total — a record in the women's game that has never been matched.

Then came the record that crossed gender lines entirely. In Brazil's group-stage match against Italy at the 2019 World Cup, Marta converted a second-half penalty for her seventeenth career World Cup goal — one more than Miroslav Klose had scored across four tournaments for Germany. It made her, outright, the leading scorer in the history of the World Cup, men's or women's, a record no one else has broken since.

She is also the only player, man or woman, to score in five separate World Cups, a run stretching from 2003 to 2019. Her sixth and final tournament came in 2023, when Brazil were eliminated in the group stage and Marta said afterward that her international career was drawing to a close. She played her farewell matches for Brazil in front of crowds that, for most of her childhood, would have told her she had no business being on the pitch at all. The law that once banned her from the game has been gone for decades. She's a large part of the reason nobody wants it back.
