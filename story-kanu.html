He Was Told His Heart Could Kill Him. Twelve Years Later, He Scored The Winner In An FA Cup Final.

FootHero Stories · Nwankwo Kanu · 6 min read

In the summer of 1996, Nwankwo Kanu was an Olympic champion at twenty years old, the toast of Nigerian football and one of the most exciting young forwards in the world. By the end of that same year, doctors were telling him he might never play professional football again.

Kanu was the standout of Nigeria's "Dream Team" at the Atlanta Olympics, a young, fearless squad that arrived with talent but little expectation. In the semifinal against Brazil, Nigeria fell behind by two goals in the second half before storming back to force extra time, where Kanu scored the golden goal that sent them to the final. Against Argentina, Nigeria again had to come from behind, eventually winning 3-2 to claim the country's first Olympic gold medal in football. Kanu, still a teenager for most of that tournament, was at the center of both comebacks.

Off the back of that performance, a move to Inter Milan was arranged. But the routine medical examination that came with the transfer turned up something nobody expected: a serious problem with one of his heart valves, the kind of defect that can be fatal if left unaddressed and put under the strain of elite competition.

Kanu underwent heart surgery in the United States rather than take the field again without treatment. For a young player who had just scored the goals of his life on the biggest stage in football, it was a brutal turn — his career, and in a very real sense his life, now depended on an operation and a recovery that offered no guarantees.

He recovered, and he returned. Kanu was back playing for Inter Milan within the same season, and by 1998 he was lifting the UEFA Cup with the club, playing on the same heart that surgeons had rebuilt less than two years earlier. It was the first sign that his career hadn't ended in that hospital — it had simply been interrupted.

A move to Arsenal followed in 1999, where Kanu became a squad player known as much for his outrageous close control as for what he'd already survived. He won the Premier League and multiple FA Cups in an Arsenal shirt, tall and gangly but capable of skill that made defenders look foolish, a reminder every time he stepped onto the pitch of how easily none of it could have happened at all.

The story found its most fitting ending years later, well past the point most forwards have retired. In the 2008 FA Cup final, playing for Portsmouth against Cardiff City, Kanu scored the only goal of the match, delivering the club its first major trophy in decades. Twelve years after doctors operated on his heart with no promises attached, he was still the man scoring the goals that mattered most.

After hanging up his boots, Kanu turned the scare that almost ended his career into a mission. The Kanu Heart Foundation, which he founded, has since worked to fund heart surgeries for children in Nigeria born with the same kind of condition that once threatened to take everything away from him.
