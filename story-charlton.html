He Survived A Plane Crash That Killed Eight Teammates. Ten Years Later, He Lifted The European Cup.

FootHero Stories · Bobby Charlton · 7 min read

On a slush-covered runway in Munich, a twenty-year-old Manchester United player was thrown from a burning plane, still strapped to his seat, and left for dead by the first people who reached the wreckage. A decade later, he stood on the Wembley turf as captain, lifting the one trophy his club had been chasing ever since.

On 6 February 1958, British European Airways Flight 609 was carrying Manchester United home from Belgrade, where the team had just secured a place in the semi-finals of the European Cup. The plane stopped to refuel in Munich. On its third attempt to take off in worsening snow, it failed to gain enough speed, skidded off the end of the runway, and broke apart. Twenty-three people died, including eight of United's players — a group of young footballers so talented and so young they'd been nicknamed the Busby Babes, after manager Matt Busby, who was himself pulled from the wreckage with injuries so severe he was given the last rites twice.

Bobby Charlton was one of the players on board. He was found still buckled into his seat, which had been thrown clear of the fuselage, and for a moment was thought by rescuers to be beyond saving. He survived with a gash to his head and, remarkably, little else. For the rest of his life, he rarely spoke about what happened on that runway in any detail — the grief of losing teammates and close friends, some of whom he'd grown up alongside in United's youth system, was not something he found easy to put into words.

What he did instead was play. Just over two weeks after the crash, United fielded a patched-together team in an FA Cup match and somehow reached that year's final. Charlton, still a teenager, was part of a squad trying to hold a shattered club together while the country watched. There was no clean recovery, no tidy turning point — just years of a young man carrying something enormous while doing the only job he knew how to do.

By 1966, Charlton had become one of the most important players in the world. Playing as an attacking midfielder for England at that summer's home World Cup, he scored twice in the semi-final win over Portugal and was named the tournament's outstanding player as England won the trophy for the only time in the nation's history. That same year, he was named European Footballer of the Year. His younger brother Jack, a defender, played alongside him in the same England side — a detail that made the achievement a family one as much as a national one.

Manchester United's own unfinished business remained the European Cup — the competition the Busby Babes had been flying home from when the crash happened. In 1968, ten years after Munich, United reached the final again, this time at Wembley against Benfica. Charlton, wearing the captain's armband, opened the scoring with a header and struck again late in extra time as United won 4-1, becoming the first English club ever to win the competition. Matt Busby, who had rebuilt the team from almost nothing twice over, watched from the touchline.

Charlton went on to become Manchester United's all-time leading goalscorer, a record that stood for parts of five decades until Wayne Rooney passed it in 2017. He was knighted for his services to football, and a statue of him stands outside Old Trafford alongside two other survivors of that era, George Best and Denis Law. When Sir Bobby Charlton died in October 2023 at the age of 86, the tributes came not just for what he won, but for what he'd carried quietly for sixty-five years to get there.
