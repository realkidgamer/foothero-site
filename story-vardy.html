Underdog

Rejected At 16. Champion At 29.

FootHero Stories · Jamie Vardy · 6 min read

At sixteen, Jamie Vardy was told he wasn't good enough. Released from his academy, he went home with no professional contract and no real plan. Thirteen years later, he'd help pull off the most unlikely title win English football has ever seen.

While most of his peers were signing their first professional deals, Vardy took a job at a factory that manufactured medical splints, playing non-league football on the side for clubs most fans have never heard of — Stocksbridge Park Steels, Halifax Town, Fleetwood Town. The pay was modest. The stage was tiny. There was no reason to believe any of it was leading anywhere.

Along the way, there were setbacks that would have ended most people's ambitions entirely. Vardy was fitted with an electronic ankle tag following an incident off the pitch, and for a period had to train and play matches while wearing it.

He kept scoring anyway.

Fleetwood Town eventually sold him to Leicester City in 2012 for a fee reported around £1 million — a non-league transfer record at the time. It looked, by most measures, like the peak of an unlikely journeyman story. Nobody was predicting what came next.

The season before Vardy's breakout, Leicester had finished bottom of the Premier League table for much of the campaign, only avoiding relegation with a dramatic late-season run. Bookmakers priced their chances of winning the title the following year at 5,000 to 1 — considered such a joke bet that it barely registered as a serious market.

Leicester went top of the table by Christmas. Vardy, playing as the team's central striker, broke the Premier League record by scoring in eleven consecutive matches, a mark that had stood since Ruud van Nistelrooy set it a decade earlier. He finished the season with 24 league goals — second only to Harry Kane across the entire division — and was named the Football Writers' Association Footballer of the Year.

Leicester finished the season with 81 points, 23 wins, and a Premier League title that remains one of the most statistically improbable outcomes in the sport's modern history. Vardy, the factory worker turned non-league journeyman turned record-breaking striker, was at the center of nearly all of it.

There's no tidy explanation for how a player released as a teenager ends up a champion at 29. Just a decade of being told no, and refusing to stop scoring anyway.
