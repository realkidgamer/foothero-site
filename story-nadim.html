Her Father Was Executed By The Taliban. She Grew Up To Become Both A Doctor And A Professional Footballer.

FootHero Stories · Nadia Nadim · 7 min read

Nadia Nadim was eleven years old when her father, a general in the Afghan army, was executed by the Taliban. Within weeks, she was fleeing the only country she'd known, smuggled across borders with her mother and four sisters toward a future none of them could have named. Two decades later, she had played professional football across two continents and qualified as a doctor — without ever putting either dream down.

The family's escape took them from Afghanistan through Pakistan and eventually into Europe, hidden for stretches of the journey in the back of trucks with no clear sense of where they'd end up. They were granted asylum in Denmark and placed in a refugee camp near the town of Randers, a country whose language none of them spoke and whose winters none of them had prepared for. Nadim has said she didn't even know where Denmark was on a map when they arrived.

It was in that camp that she found football. She saw local kids kicking a ball around near the housing and joined in, and unlike everything else in her new life, this needed no translation. She was good at it almost immediately. Within a few years she was playing for a local Danish club, then a senior side, then pulling on the national team shirt — becoming a full Denmark international by her early twenties.

What followed was a career that took her almost everywhere the women's game was played. She won a domestic double in Denmark, then crossed the Atlantic to the United States, helping Portland Thorns to an NWSL championship. From there it was Manchester City, then Paris Saint-Germain, where she scored 18 goals in 27 appearances and helped the club win its first-ever French league title in 2021. She has since played in Kentucky, Italy, and Sweden, adding caps and goals for Denmark all the while — more than 100 appearances and dozens of goals across a career that never really stopped moving.

Because the whole time she was building that footballing résumé, she was also enrolled at Aarhus University, working toward a medical degree with an eye on becoming a reconstructive surgeon. For five years she split her time between training grounds, airports, and lecture halls, fitting clinical placements around international breaks and studying between matches. In January 2022, she graduated and became a qualified doctor — still an active professional athlete the entire time.

She earned her medical degree during rehab from a torn ACL. The injury had struck her right knee the previous September, and while she worked to get back onto the pitch, she was also finishing the coursework that made her Dr. Nadim. She returned to competitive football months later — and then, almost exactly a year after the first injury, tore the ACL in her other knee. She rehabbed that one too, and came back again the following season.

Nadim has since become an ambassador for the Danish Refugee Council, using her own story to advocate for people going through what her family went through. She's often described as speaking close to a dozen languages, a reminder of just how many places this one journey has passed through. There's no single word for a life that includes fleeing a war, learning a new one from scratch, becoming an international athlete, and qualifying as a surgeon — all before turning forty. Nadim has simply kept doing all of it at once.
