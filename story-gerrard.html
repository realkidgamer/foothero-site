Comeback

3-0 Down At Half-Time. European Champions By Midnight.

FootHero Stories · Steven Gerrard · 7 min read

Steven Gerrard grew up a few miles from Anfield, went through Liverpool's academy as a boy, and captained the club he'd supported his whole life. On the night of the 2005 Champions League final in Istanbul, he watched his team fall apart in the first half — and then decided he wasn't going to let it stay that way.

AC Milan were relentless from the opening whistle. Paolo Maldini scored inside the first minute, heading in from a corner before most of the stadium had settled into their seats. Hernán Crespo added two more before the break — a close-range finish and then a delicate chip that Liverpool's goalkeeper, Jerzy Dudek, could only watch sail over him. By half-time it was 3-0. Commentators were already discussing where this Milan side ranked among the greatest teams in European football history.

In the Liverpool dressing room, there wasn't much time to feel sorry for themselves. Manager Rafael Benítez reorganized the team's shape, switching to three at the back and introducing a fresh midfielder in Dietmar Hamann to shore things up. But the tone in that room was set as much by its captain as by its manager. Gerrard, born and raised in Huyton just outside the city, wasn't ready to let the biggest match of his career end as a rout.

Six minutes changed everything. In the 54th minute, Gerrard rose above Milan's defense to head home a cross from John Arne Riise, then wheeled away pumping his fist, screaming at his own bench to believe. Two minutes later, Vladimír Šmicer drilled a shot in from the edge of the box to make it 3-2. Then, in the 60th minute, Xabi Alonso had his initial penalty saved by Milan's goalkeeper — and calmly buried the rebound. Anfield's away end, and a good portion of neutral Istanbul, had gone completely delirious. In less time than it takes to watch a sitcom episode, Liverpool had erased a three-goal deficit in a European Cup final.

The comeback still needed finishing off. In extra time, Dudek produced a double save in the dying seconds to deny Andriy Shevchenko from point-blank range, keeping the score at 3-3 and forcing a penalty shootout. Liverpool converted three of their four spot-kicks; Dudek saved Shevchenko's decisive attempt to seal it. Liverpool had won their fifth European Cup, and Gerrard — the local kid who'd come through the club's youth ranks — climbed the steps of the Atatürk Olympic Stadium to lift the trophy as captain.

The match has been remembered ever since as the Miracle of Istanbul, and Gerrard's header is usually cited as the moment the impossible started to feel possible. It wasn't a season-long redemption arc or a comeback from injury — it was six minutes, in one match, where a captain who'd supported the club since he was a boy refused to accept what the scoreboard was telling him.
