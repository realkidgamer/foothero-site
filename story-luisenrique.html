Playing For Her: Luis Enrique's Quest to Win It All For His Daughter

FootHero Stories · Luis Enrique · 5 min read

In 2015, a nine-year-old girl ran onto the Olympiastadion pitch in Berlin and planted a Barcelona flag into the turf, celebrating alongside her father as he lifted the Champions League trophy. Ten years later, he would try to recreate that exact moment — without her.

Xana Martínez was the daughter of Luis Enrique, then managing Barcelona to a historic treble. The image of her celebrating with her father became one of the most fondly remembered moments of that title run — a small, human detail inside a huge sporting achievement.

In August 2019, while serving as manager of Spain's national team, Luis Enrique stepped away from the role. His daughter had been diagnosed with osteosarcoma, a rare and aggressive form of bone cancer. Five months later, at just nine years old, Xana passed away.

Enrique returned to coaching, eventually taking charge of Paris Saint-Germain. Along the way, he and his wife Elena Cullell established the Xana Foundation, supporting children and families facing similarly difficult circumstances — turning private grief into something that could help others.

By May 2025, PSG had reached their first-ever Champions League final, facing Inter Milan in Munich. Before the match, Enrique spoke openly about the significance of the occasion, describing how he had once planted a flag on the pitch alongside Xana after that 2015 win — and how, physically, she wouldn't be there to do it again.

PSG produced one of the most dominant performances in the competition's history, beating Inter 5-0 to win the club's first Champions League title. But the moment that spread furthest afterward had little to do with the scoreline. As the final whistle blew, PSG supporters unveiled a giant tifo depicting Enrique and Xana together. Enrique changed into a t-shirt bearing an illustration of his daughter planting a flag into the ground, and wore it as he collected his winner's medal.

"She is always with us. We carry her forever in our hearts."

Even Real Madrid, one of PSG's fiercest rivals, publicly acknowledged the tribute in their congratulatory message — a small sign of how far the moment traveled beyond football itself.

Luis Enrique has now won the Champions League with two different clubs, a decade apart, each time with his daughter close by in some form — first in person, then in memory. Some victories are about trophies. This one, for him, was never really about that at all.
