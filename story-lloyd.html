Redemption

She Was Cut From The Youth Team. Sixteen Minutes Into A World Cup Final, She'd Scored A Hat-Trick.

FootHero Stories · Carli Lloyd · 6 min read

As a teenager, Carli Lloyd was cut from the U.S. Under-21 national team and seriously considered walking away from soccer for good. Twelve years later, in the biggest match the sport offers, she scored three goals before most fans had finished finding their seats.

The cut stung in a specific way. Lloyd had grown up as one of the most talented youth players in New Jersey, the kind of kid coaches assumed was destined for the national team as a matter of course. Getting left off it didn't just hurt — it forced a real question about whether she was good enough to keep going at all.

She kept going, but only after changing almost everything about how she trained. She started working with a private coach, James Galanis, who rebuilt her approach to the game from the ground up — extra fitness sessions, extra shooting drills, hours added onto an already full schedule. The partnership was less about raw talent, which she'd always had, and more about turning her into someone who could be trusted with the ball in the moments that mattered most.

That trust got tested early and often, and Lloyd kept passing. At the 2008 Beijing Olympics, with the U.S. and Brazil scoreless deep into extra time of the gold medal match, it was Lloyd who found the goal that won it. Four years later in London, facing Japan in the Olympic final again, she scored twice to help the U.S. win gold a second time. By the time the 2015 World Cup arrived in Canada, "big-game Carli" wasn't a nickname anymore — it was a track record.

She scored three goals before most fans had finished finding their seats.

Even with that history, nothing quite prepared anyone for the World Cup final against Japan. Lloyd opened the scoring in the third minute, added a second in the fifth, and then, in the sixteenth minute, spotted the Japanese goalkeeper off her line and lofted a shot from inside her own half that dropped into the net. Three goals, sixteen minutes, one World Cup final — a hat-trick faster than anything the tournament had ever seen, in either the men's or women's game.

The U.S. went on to win the final 5-2, claiming its first World Cup title in sixteen years. Lloyd was named the tournament's best player and, a few months later, was named FIFA's World Player of the Year — an honor she'd repeat the following year. But the trophies were only ever the visible part of the story. The real substance was in the years nobody was watching: a teenager told she wasn't good enough, choosing to get better instead of getting out.

There's a version of Carli Lloyd's career that ends at the U-21 cut — a promising player who didn't make it, a name that fades from the local papers. Instead there's this one: a player who turned rejection into a training plan, and a training plan into the biggest sixteen minutes women's soccer has ever seen.
