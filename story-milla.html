He'd Retired From The National Team. At 42, He Became The Oldest Man To Ever Score At A World Cup.

FootHero Stories · Roger Milla · 6 min read

By 1990, Roger Milla was thirty-eight years old, retired from the Cameroon national team, and playing club football on the French island of Réunion — a quiet, unglamorous stage a long way from a World Cup. Then the president of his country picked up the phone.

Cameroon's coaching staff hadn't planned on selecting him. Milla had walked away from international football two years earlier, and by most footballing logic his best days were behind him. But President Paul Biya personally requested that the federation add Milla to the squad for Italia '90, and the request was not one the federation was in a position to refuse. Milla flew in as a late addition to a team nobody expected to do much of anything.

Cameroon opened the tournament by beating the defending champions. Argentina, with Diego Maradona in the number ten shirt, lost 1-0 to a Cameroon side playing in its first World Cup match in eight years. Milla didn't start that game — he'd been brought in as an impact substitute, a veteran to introduce once matches opened up — but the upset put the rest of the world on notice that something unusual was happening in Cameroon's group.

It was in the round of 16 against Colombia that Milla became a global story in his own right. Brought on again from the bench, he scored twice in extra time, the second after dispossessing goalkeeper René Higuita, who had wandered far from his goal with the ball at his feet. The two goals sent Cameroon through to the quarterfinals — the first time any African nation had ever reached that stage of a World Cup.

At forty-two years old, he became the oldest man to ever score at a World Cup.

Each time Milla scored, he ran to the corner flag and danced with it, hips swinging, grinning at the cameras. It became one of the most replayed celebrations in World Cup history — a moment of pure, unguarded joy from a man twice the age of some of his opponents, and it made him one of the most beloved figures of the tournament.

Cameroon's run ended in the quarterfinals, where they twice took the lead against England before losing 3-2 in extra time. Milla finished the tournament with four goals, and at thirty-eight, he'd become the oldest goalscorer in World Cup history. It seemed, reasonably, like the end of his story.

It wasn't. Four years later, at forty-two, Milla was recalled again for USA '94. Cameroon's campaign was largely miserable — they were eliminated in the group stage and suffered a heavy 6-1 defeat to Russia — but Milla came off the bench in that match and scored. It broke his own record. At forty-two years old, he became the oldest man to ever score at a World Cup, a mark that still stands today. Twice retired, twice recalled, twice a record-breaker — Roger Milla simply refused to be finished.
