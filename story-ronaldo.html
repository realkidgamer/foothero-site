Carried Off In Tears. Still Lifted The Trophy.

FootHero Stories · Cristiano Ronaldo · 6 min read

Twelve years earlier, a 19-year-old Cristiano Ronaldo had stood on home soil and cried after losing a European Championship final his country was supposed to win. In 2016, in Paris, he cried again in a European Championship final — this time nine minutes in, being helped off the pitch on a stretcher of arms before his team had even settled into the game.

Portugal had not made it to the final easily. They drew all three of their group matches at Euro 2016, including a wild 3-3 draw with Hungary, and only advanced because of the tournament's expanded format for third-placed teams. From there they beat Croatia in extra time, edged past Poland on penalties in the quarterfinal, and knocked out Wales in the semifinal behind a Ronaldo header. Nobody outside the Portuguese camp was picking them to win the whole thing.

Then, in the final against France at the Stade de France, Ronaldo went down in a collision with Dimitri Payet early in the first half. He tried to play through it, wincing his way through a handful of minutes, before the pain forced him to try again and fail. Around the 25th minute, in tears, the captain of Portugal was substituted off the biggest stage of his international career, replaced by a teenage Renato Sanches.

He couldn't play anymore. So he started coaching instead.

What happened next became almost as memorable as the injury itself. Unable to influence the match with his feet, Ronaldo spent the rest of the final on the touchline acting as an auxiliary coach — pacing the technical area, clapping his teammates on, shouting instructions, pulling players in for words between passages of play. Cameras kept finding him marshaling a back line and midfield he could no longer join.

The final stayed goalless through ninety minutes and into extra time, tense and scrappy, decided by fine margins. In the 109th minute, substitute Éder struck a long-range shot past French goalkeeper Hugo Lloris. It was the only goal of the match. Portugal held on, and when the final whistle blew, they had won 1-0 — the country's first ever major international trophy, on French soil, without their best player on the pitch for most of it.

Ronaldo, on crutches, still lifted the trophy alongside his teammates. The image that lingered wasn't of a superstar scoring the winner — it was of a captain who couldn't play, refusing to let that stop him from leading anyway. Twelve years after crying over a final his country lost, he cried over one they finally won.
