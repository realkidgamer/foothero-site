He Fractured His Skull On A Football Pitch. He Never Played Without A Helmet Again.

FootHero Stories · Petr Cech · 6 min read

On an October evening at Reading's Madejski Stadium, a routine dive at an opponent's feet nearly cost Petr Cech his life. Ten weeks later, he was back between the posts for Chelsea — wearing a padded black helmet he would never take off again.

By the autumn of 2006, Cech was already the best goalkeeper in England. Since arriving at Chelsea in 2004, he had anchored José Mourinho's back line through two Premier League titles, setting a clean-sheet standard that reshaped what fans expected from a keeper. He was 24 years old, at the peak of his sport, with nothing about that Saturday's fixture at newly promoted Reading suggesting it would become the defining afternoon of his career.

Early in the match, Cech dove low to smother the ball at the feet of Reading midfielder Stephen Hunt. The two collided, Hunt's knee driving into the side of Cech's head. Cech got up, took a few steps, and collapsed. He was stretchered off and rushed to hospital, where scans showed a depressed fracture of the skull. He went into emergency surgery that same night.

The injury was serious enough that it has since been described, including by Cech himself, as life-threatening. He spent weeks out of football entirely, his season and his long-term health both in question. There was no guarantee he would ever play top-flight football again, let alone return to the level that had made him one of the most feared shot-stoppers in the world.

Cech returned on December 23, 2006, starting against Liverpool at Stamford Bridge in a padded, rugby-style headguard designed to protect the site of the fracture. It looked strange at first — unlike anything supporters were used to seeing on a football pitch. Within a season, it wasn't strange at all. It was simply what Petr Cech looked like.

He never played another match without the helmet.

He kept it on for the rest of his career. Across the years that followed, Cech helped Chelsea win further Premier League titles, FA Cups, a League Cup, and, in 2012, the Champions League — the first in the club's history — before adding the Europa League the following year. In 2015 he moved across London to Arsenal, still wearing the same style of protective headgear, and went on to lift more FA Cups in red and white.

His career ended, fittingly, on the biggest stage against the club where it had nearly ended thirteen years earlier. In the 2019 Europa League final in Baku, Cech started in goal for Arsenal against Chelsea — his last-ever professional match. Chelsea won the game, but the symmetry belonged to Cech: the goalkeeper who had been carried off a pitch in genuine danger was walking off one, on his own terms, at the end of a career few thought was guaranteed to continue at all.
