Born In A Refugee Camp. Champion Of Europe At 19.

FootHero Stories · Alphonso Davies · 6 min read

Alphonso Davies was born in a refugee camp in Ghana, to Liberian parents who had fled a civil war with nothing. Nineteen years later, he started a Champions League final for Bayern Munich and helped his team win it.

His parents, Debeah and Victoria Davies, left Liberia during the country's brutal civil conflict and found shelter at the Buduburam refugee camp, a sprawling settlement outside Accra that housed tens of thousands of Liberians with nowhere else to go. It was there, in November 2000, that Alphonso was born — a child of displacement, in a camp built for people who had lost everything.

When Alphonso was five, the family was resettled to Canada, first to Windsor, Ontario, and then to Edmonton, Alberta. They arrived with little, and Alphonso grew up in a low-income neighborhood on the city's north side, chasing a ball around local parks and community pitches in a country where soccer barely registered next to hockey.

It didn't take long for coaches to notice how different he was. Davies was fast in a way that looked almost unfair for a kid his age, and by his early teens he'd been pulled into the Vancouver Whitecaps' residency academy. At fifteen, he signed a homegrown contract and made his Major League Soccer debut, becoming one of the youngest players ever to appear in the league.

Bayern Munich came calling in January 2019, signing him for a fee reported around $22 million — a record for a Canadian player at the time, for a teenager who had been playing in front of a few thousand fans in Vancouver less than three years earlier. He finished that season on loan back with the Whitecaps before officially reporting to Bayern, where coach Hansi Flick made an unusual decision: he moved Davies, a winger his whole career, to left-back.

A camp built for people who had lost everything. A final that decided who was best in Europe.

The switch worked immediately. Davies became one of the most electric fullbacks in the world, his recovery speed turning defensive disasters into non-events and his surging runs down the flank becoming a signature of Bayern's play. In the 2019-20 season, Bayern Munich won the treble — the Bundesliga, the DFB-Pokal, and the Champions League — and Davies started the final against Paris Saint-Germain at just nineteen years old, one of the youngest players to start a Champions League final in the competition's modern history. Bayern won 1-0.

He'd become a Canadian citizen only a few years earlier, in 2017, the same year he made his senior debut for Canada's men's national team. He went on to captain the program through its long-awaited return to the World Cup in 2022, the country's first appearance in the tournament since 1986. In 2023, a diagnosis of myocarditis — inflammation of the heart muscle — sidelined him for months and put his career in question. He came back anyway, the same way he'd done everything else: by refusing to stay down.

There is no scouting report for a refugee camp. Nobody drafts a five-year-old with nothing, in a country he'd never seen, and predicts a Champions League trophy. Davies' story isn't a straight line from hardship to glory — it's a reminder that greatness sometimes starts in places built only to survive.
