She Tore Her ACL The Day Before The Euros. A Year Later, She Lifted The World Cup.

FootHero Stories · Alexia Putellas · 6 min read

In the summer of 2022, Alexia Putellas was the best footballer on the planet, back-to-back Ballon d'Or in hand, about to lead Spain into a home Euros she'd spent her whole career waiting for. Then, in a routine training session the day before the tournament opened, her left knee gave out.

Putellas had come up through Barcelona's youth system and spent years as the connective tissue of a midfield that reshaped what was possible for a women's club side, controlling matches with a passing range and game intelligence that drew comparisons to the best midfielders in the men's game. In 2021 she won the inaugural Ballon d'Or Féminin. In 2022 she won it again, becoming the first player to retain the award. There was no argument left to have about who the sport's best player was.

Then came July 5, 2022 — the day before Spain's opening match of the European Championship on home soil against Finland. In training, Putellas planted awkwardly and went down clutching her left knee. Scans confirmed the worst: a torn ACL. Her knee buckled, and the tournament she'd built her whole career toward was over before it started. Spain went into the Euros without their captain and talisman, eventually falling to England, the eventual champions, in the quarterfinals.

Her knee buckled, and the tournament she'd built her whole career toward was over before it started.

What followed was surgery and the unglamorous part of the sport nobody puts on a highlight reel: months of rebuilding a knee joint by joint, rebuilding trust in a leg that had once betrayed her without warning, and watching her team compete for a trophy from the sidelines. ACL recoveries at the elite level typically run the better part of a year, and Putellas's was no exception — a long stretch spent in gyms and rehab pools while the rest of the sport moved on without her.

She worked her way back into Barcelona's side the following season, easing back into competitive minutes as her fitness returned. By the summer of 2023, she'd done enough to be named in Spain's squad for the World Cup in Australia and New Zealand — not yet the dominant, every-minute force she'd been before the injury, but back in a Spain shirt at a major tournament for the first time since the operation.

Spain went on to win it. On August 20, 2023, in Sydney, they beat England 1-0 in the final on a goal from Olga Carmona, delivering the country's first Women's World Cup title. Putellas featured across the tournament in a rotated, carefully managed role as she continued building back toward full match sharpness — not the headline story of the run, but unmistakably part of it, on the pitch and in the celebration when the trophy was finally lifted.

A year earlier, her tournament had ended before it began. This time, she got to finish it.
