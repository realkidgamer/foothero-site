Down 2-1 In Stoppage Time. He Ended Manchester City's 44-Year Wait For A Title.

FootHero Stories · Sergio Agüero · 6 min read

With the clock past ninety minutes and Manchester City trailing at home to a team fighting off relegation, the Premier League title was slipping away in the cruelest possible way. Sergio Agüero needed less than two minutes to make sure it didn't.

Manchester City had not been champions of England since 1968. In between, the club spent long stretches outside the top flight entirely, a punchline more often than a contender, before a 2008 takeover poured serious investment into rebuilding it into a title challenger. By the final day of the 2011-12 season, that rebuild had produced a genuine title race: City and Manchester United arrived level on points, with City ahead only on goal difference. Anything less than victory over Queens Park Rangers at the Etihad risked handing United the championship instead.

The afternoon did not go to plan. QPR, needing points themselves to avoid relegation, took an early lead. City equalized, but the match only grew stranger from there — QPR had a player sent off yet somehow went back in front through Jamie Mackie in the second half, leaving City down 2-1 despite playing against ten men, while word spread around the ground that United were beating Sunderland to keep the pressure on.

Ninety minutes came and went, and City were still losing. By several supporters' own later accounts, some had already begun heading for the exits, resigned to watching a title race they had led for most of the season slip away in its final moments.

Then, in the second minute of stoppage time, Edin Džeko rose to head home a corner and level the score at 2-2. A draw was no longer good enough. With United already leading, City needed a winner, and they needed it before the referee blew for full time.

Ninety minutes came and went, and City were still losing.

It came almost immediately. Mario Balotelli slid a pass into the box, Sergio Agüero took a touch to bring it under control, and from just inside the penalty area he drove the ball into the bottom corner. The goal landed with the game clock reading 93:20 — a number that has stayed attached to the moment ever since in English football memory, alongside commentator Martin Tyler's call of "Agueroooooo!", replayed almost as often as the goal itself.

Manchester City won 3-2. On goal difference alone, they were champions of England for the first time since 1968 — a title race that had run for nine months, settled in the space of roughly ninety seconds right at the end of it. Agüero went on to become the club's all-time leading scorer before he left nearly a decade later, but no goal he ever scored carries the same weight as the one that arrived with almost no time left to spare.
