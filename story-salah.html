Chelsea Let Him Go Without A Fight. The Rest Of The League Voted Him Its Best Player.

FootHero Stories · Mohamed Salah · 7 min read

In January 2014, Chelsea signed a 21-year-old winger from Basel named Mohamed Salah. Jose Mourinho barely used him, loaned him out twice, and let him leave for good within two and a half years. A decade later, the club that couldn't find a place for him would watch him get voted the best player in the league he now dominates — and use the money he made doing it to rebuild the village he grew up in.

Salah's spell at Chelsea never got going. Mourinho used him sparingly and out of position, and in two full seasons on the books, Salah never scored a single Premier League goal for the club. He was loaned to Fiorentina in 2015, then to Roma for the following season, with Chelsea eventually making the Roma move permanent in 2016 and writing off the transfer as a loss. It looked, at the time, like a promising young player who simply hadn't worked out in England.

Roma is where the player Liverpool would later sign started to take shape. Over two seasons in Serie A, Salah became one of the most dangerous wide forwards in Italy, a key attacking outlet for a Roma side that finished as Serie A runners-up in 2016-17. Liverpool paid a club-record fee for him that summer — a gamble, by the standards of a player Chelsea had just let go for barely more than a shrug.

He didn't need time to adjust. In his first season at Anfield, Salah scored 32 league goals — matching the record for a 38-game Premier League season — and won the Golden Boot outright. He was named the PFA Players' Player of the Year, English football's peer-selected honor for its best performer. The professionals who spent the season trying to stop him voted him the best player in the league anyway.

More followed. Salah scored the opening goal — a penalty inside the first two minutes — as Liverpool beat Tottenham Hotspur in the 2019 Champions League final. The next season, Liverpool won the Premier League title for the first time in thirty years, with Salah again among the division's top scorers. He has gone on to collect the Golden Boot more than once since that debut campaign, output that has barely dipped across the better part of a decade at the club.

None of it stayed in Merseyside. Salah has spent much of what his career has earned him on Nagrig, the small town in Egypt's Nile Delta where he grew up. He funded a wastewater treatment plant for the town after years of poor sanitation infrastructure, helped pay for improvements to the local school, donated an ambulance, and has repeatedly given to hospitals and medical causes across Egypt, including support during the country's COVID-19 response. In Liverpool, fans of every background sing his name in the stands; in Nagrig, he's the reason the town has running services most Liverpool supporters take for granted.

Chelsea decided, twice, that a place couldn't be found for him — first by keeping him on the bench, then by selling him without much of a fight. Everyone who has lined up against him since, and the town he never stopped sending money home to, would tell a different story.
