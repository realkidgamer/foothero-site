Sacrifice

His Cousin Gave Him Part Of A Liver. His Captain Gave Him The Armband.

FootHero Stories · Eric Abidal · 5 min read

In March 2011, Eric Abidal was told he had a tumor on his liver. Barely two months later, he stood in the tunnel at Wembley before a Champions League final, and Barcelona's captain took off his own armband and handed it to him instead.

Abidal was never the player who made highlight reels. A French left-back who came through at Lyon before joining Barcelona in 2007, he built his reputation on positioning, recovery pace, and the kind of quiet reliability that let the more famous names in front of him do their work. By the time he was diagnosed, he had already won league titles and a Champions League with the club and settled in as one of the most trusted defenders in the world.

The diagnosis came out of nowhere. A tumor was found on his liver, and he underwent surgery in Barcelona to have it removed. For a professional athlete in his early thirties, the word alone was enough to end a career, let alone a season. Teammates and staff at the club have spoken since about how sudden and frightening the news felt inside the dressing room.

Recovery came faster than anyone had reason to expect. Within weeks, Abidal was training again and traveling with the squad as Barcelona prepared to face Manchester United in the 2011 Champions League final at Wembley. Nobody would have blamed him for staying home and watching from a hospital bed. Instead, he was back among his teammates for the biggest match of the year.

Barcelona won that final 3-1, but the moment that outlasted the scoreline came in the minutes after the final whistle. Carles Puyol, the club's long-time captain, took off his own armband and put it on Abidal's arm instead, so that it would be Abidal — not Puyol — who lifted the trophy first in front of the world. It remains one of the most replayed images the Champions League has ever produced.

The story didn't end there, and it didn't end happily for a while. The following year, his condition worsened again, and doctors determined he would need a liver transplant. His cousin turned out to be a match and donated part of his own liver so the surgery could go ahead — an operation far more serious, and far more permanent, than the one that had sidelined him in 2011.

Once again, Abidal came back. Only months after the transplant, he returned to the Barcelona squad and played in the 2012 Copa del Rey final, which the club went on to win. He never became the kind of player remembered for goals or trophies won alone. He's remembered for something rarer — a defender who needed a piece of a family member's liver to keep playing the sport he loved, and still found his way back onto the biggest stages in the game.
