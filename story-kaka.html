Doctors Said He Might Never Walk Right Again. Seven Years Later, He Was The Best Player On Earth.

FootHero Stories · Kaká · 6 min read

In September 2000, an eighteen-year-old Brazilian named Ricardo Kaká dove into the shallow end of a swimming pool and fractured a vertebra in his spine. For a few frightening days, nobody could say for certain whether he'd walk normally again, let alone play professional football. Seven years later, he was holding the Champions League trophy and the Ballon d'Or in the same season.

Kaká was already considered one of the brightest young talents in São Paulo FC's academy when the accident happened, during a break spent with his family. The fall was ordinary — the kind of moment that happens at pools all over the world without consequence. This time it wasn't. He was rushed to hospital with a spinal injury serious enough that specialists raised the possibility of permanent damage, including the risk that he might never play sport again.

What followed was months of uncertainty rather than a single decisive surgery. Kaká avoided an operation altogether, instead working through a long, cautious course of physiotherapy and rest while doctors monitored whether the fracture would heal on its own. He has spoken often in the years since about how frightened he was in that period, and about leaning on his faith and his family to get through it — a devotion to his Christian beliefs that would go on to define how the public saw him for the rest of his career.

He recovered fully, returned to training, and made his professional debut for São Paulo FC in 2001. Within two years he was one of the most exciting attacking midfielders in Brazilian football, and in 2003 AC Milan signed him — a transfer that would end up looking like one of the great bargains of the decade once his career took off in Italy.

The body specialists once feared might never let him live a normal life went on to be crowned the finest in the game.

Milan's rise with Kaká in the side wasn't instant redemption. In 2005, he and his teammates were minutes from a comfortable Champions League title in Istanbul before Liverpool produced one of the most famous comebacks in the competition's history to force penalties and win the trophy. Two years later, Milan met Liverpool again in the final, this time in Athens — and won 2-1, with Kaká playing a starring role throughout that season's Champions League campaign.

That same year, 2007, he was named FIFA World Player of the Year and won the Ballon d'Or, becoming one of the small handful of players ever to hold both the world's biggest individual football honors and a Champions League winner's medal in the same twelve months. When the final whistle blew in Athens, he lifted his jersey to reveal a t-shirt underneath reading "I Belong to Jesus" — a message he'd carry through the rest of a career built, by his own account, on faith as much as football.

The body specialists once feared might never let him live a normal life went on to be crowned the finest in the game. Kaká rarely tells the story of 2000 as a footnote to his success — he tells it as the reason he never took a single minute of it for granted.
